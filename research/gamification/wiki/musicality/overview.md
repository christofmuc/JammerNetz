# Musicality dimensions

This section defines the behaviors the game should encourage before deciding
how to measure them.

## Pulse and groove

- Maintain a stable relation to the perceived beat.
- Distinguish stable personal microtiming from random timing variance.
- Recover after drift and support the pulse when the ensemble weakens.
- Share accents and subdivision without demanding metronomic uniformity.

## Form and phrasing

- Enter, stop, fill, and return at meaningful bar and section boundaries.
- Think in two-, four-, and eight-bar phrases.
- Create ensemble breaths and dynamic arcs.

## Motif, consistency, and variety

- Establish recognizable rhythmic or melodic material.
- Repeat it sufficiently to create identity.
- Develop one dimension while preserving others.
- Avoid both unchanging wallpaper and unrelated novelty.

## Harmony and tuning

- Relate notes to the current harmonic context.
- Distinguish stability, tension, and resolution rather than only
  diatonic/non-diatonic membership.
- Use voice leading, guide tones, bass motion, bends, and tuning intentionally.

## Listening and interaction

- Leave space, hand over attention, answer another player, and adapt density or
  dynamics.
- Reinforce or complement another role rather than merely maximize activity.
- Recognize leadership, following, rescue, and recovery.

## Balance and role

- Make an audible contribution appropriate to a declared or inferred role.
- Avoid persistent spectral masking and uncontrolled level dominance.
- Do not judge pads, drums, bass, comping, lead, vocals, and texture by one
  universal event-density target.

## Open question

The central evaluation problem is not whether a feature correlates with a
number. It is whether musicians agree that rewarding the feature improves the
jam. Human review therefore remains part of every productization gate.
