# Game design and feedback

## Feedback hierarchy

1. A cooperative connection meter describes the ensemble.
2. Private coaching helps an individual recover without public embarrassment.
3. Shared achievements celebrate audible interaction.
4. Post-session explanations show the evidence behind awards and warnings.

## Candidate mechanics

- Pulse halo, pocket fingerprint, drift warning, and recovery combo.
- Land the One, phrase landing, group breath, and fill license.
- Hook established, hook confirmed, same-but-different, and four-bar architect.
- Call and response, conversation baton, space bonus, and rescue bonus.
- Bass-kick handshake, accent agreement, dynamic arc, and spectrum tetris.
- Chord gravity, guide-tone landing, tension-resolution, and tuning constellation.

## Scoring guardrails

- Use multiple independent dimensions instead of one opaque total score.
- Cap rewards that can be farmed through event density or silence.
- Learn stable personal timing bias before judging variance.
- Weight by role, musical context, and analyzer confidence.
- Reward recovery and adaptation, not only uninterrupted streaks.
- Preserve an `unknown` state and explain why evidence was insufficient.

## Live visualization

- Green: stable and confident relationship.
- Blue: stable offbeat or personal pocket.
- Amber: weakening confidence or emerging drift.
- Red: sustained high-confidence loss of pulse, never a single event.
- Grey: silence or insufficient evidence.

The exact color semantics remain a design hypothesis until musicians test them.
Most feedback should update on beats or bars rather than flicker at audio-frame
rate.
