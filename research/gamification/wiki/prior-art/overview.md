# Prior-art strategy

The purpose of prior-art work is to accelerate the playable gamification
experiment, not to turn JammerNetz into an unconstrained MIR research program.

## Search axes

- onset, beat, downbeat, tempo, meter, groove, and microtiming;
- chord, key, chroma, tuning, transcription, and score following;
- motif, repetition, structure, interaction, and co-creativity;
- networked music performance, clock synchronization, and latency;
- music education, feedback, visualization, and gamification;
- datasets, evaluation methods, reproducible software, and licensing.

## Venue order

1. Sound and Music Computing (SMC) as the initial bounded MIR corpus.
2. NIME as a complementary source for collaborative interaction, feedback,
   learning, accessibility, and evaluation design.
3. Citation expansion into ISMIR, ICMC, DAFx, AES, and ICASSP.
4. Only then broaden to generic machine-learning search where music-specific
   evidence is absent.

## Disposition

Every relevant work receives one disposition:

- `adopt`: directly evaluable component or artifact;
- `adapt`: useful method requiring JammerNetz-specific work;
- `reference`: informs design or evaluation but is not an implementation path;
- `defer`: relevant but not on the shortest playable path;
- `reject`: unsuitable, with a recorded reason.

See [SMC proceedings coverage](smc.md) for aggregate counters and the
[SMC 2025 screening report](smc-2025-screening.md) for the first complete-year
pilot. See [NIME proceedings discovery source](nime.md) for the expected value,
scope limits, and proposed metadata pilot for that venue.
